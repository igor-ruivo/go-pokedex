About GO Pokédex

GO Pokédex is a free companion for Pokémon GO players: live events and raids, PvP and raid rankings, a team builder that rates trios and finds the best teams in your own collection, and search strings that clean your storage. It is built to be quick to use mid-game.

The story

I'm Igor, a software engineer and a bit of an optimization obsessive.

This project started in the summer of 2023 as a way for me to teach myself React, CSS, and modern frontend development. At the time, I was getting seriously into Pokémon GO PvP and discovering how much depth was hidden underneath what initially seemed like a simple mobile game. IVs, CP caps, rankings, movesets, leagues, and all the strange optimization around them completely hooked me.

I quickly found myself relying on the excellent tools that the Pokémon GO community had already built: PvPoke for PvP analysis and rankings, Poké Genie and PVPIVs for IVs, LeekDuck for game information, PokéMiners for game data, and others. These projects are fantastic at what they do, and this project isn't meant to replace them or recreate their work. It builds on top of it.

What I wanted to create was the all-in-one companion experience that I personally couldn't find: dense, information-rich, fast, mobile-oriented, and with the kind of UX/UI attention to detail that I enjoy designing myself. But the biggest difference is that I wanted the tool to be centred around your own Pokémon, rather than just Pokémon GO as a whole.

Your Pokémon, not just Pokémon

The Team Builder is inspired by the excellent work of PvPoke, including its battle simulation and ranking algorithms, but takes the idea further by connecting it to your own collection. You can keep a personal list of interesting league-oriented Pokémon, and the tool can continuously work out which team combinations make the most sense from what you actually own. Teams are ranked against the current meta and accompanied by useful analysis and tips.

That means your collection becomes something persistent rather than something you have to manually reassess every season. When moves, buffs, nerfs, new Pokémon, or the meta change, you can come back and immediately see which of your Pokémon and teams have become more or less relevant.

Save your Pokémon once. Let the tool keep figuring out what they are good for.

Search strings that actually do the work

Another major part of the project is automated Pokémon GO search strings. My favourite example is the non-perfect-IV mass deletion search. The tool can generate an enormous search string covering the relevant league configurations you've selected and account for things like evolution lines, shadows, purification, Best Buddy, and the different optimal IV spreads for each species.

The result is something I personally find transformative: instead of manually checking hundreds of Pokémon, I can confidently bulk-delete the ones that cannot possibly be interesting for my selected leagues and only inspect the small number of survivors that are genuinely worth looking at.

It also works in the other direction. The tool can help preserve potentially valuable Pokémon that aren't currently meta relevant, such as top-ranked Great or Ultra League candidates that I would otherwise have deleted because I had no practical way of checking every Pokémon in my storage. There are also searches for trade candidates, where the tool picks out Pokémon whose IV requirements make sense specifically because a Best Friend trade guarantees a minimum IV floor.

Built on the community

Underneath all of this, I rely heavily on the existing Pokémon GO data ecosystem. My backend parses PokéMiners' Game Master and translation data and regularly consumes information from Pokémon GO's official channels and established community sources such as LeekDuck. PvP analysis builds on PvPoke's battle simulation work rather than attempting to reinvent it. A lot of what makes this project possible is thanks to people who have already spent years building these tools and datasets.

I'm essentially trying to add another layer on top: a richer interface, a more personal experience, and more automation around the Pokémon you actually own. The project has also been my playground for learning frontend development, experimenting with increasingly capable AI coding tools, and obsessing over UX/UI details for far longer than I originally intended.

Now I'd like to give some of that work back to the community. If you use the tool, I'd genuinely love feedback, criticism, ideas, bug reports, or suggestions for things that could be better. The project is still evolving, and I'd rather have the community help shape it than build everything inside my own bubble.

Igor

Privacy

There are no accounts. Your settings, collection and favorite teams stay in your browser and never leave your device. We use Google Analytics to count visits anonymously; it may set cookies. There are no ads.
